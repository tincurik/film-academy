# Film craft playbook

43 techniques for product, launch and brand films, distilled from motion designers, editors, studios, ad research and papers (each claim keeps its source link). Use it at three points: **before** you write the film (structure, one promise, references), **while** you build (picture, editing, sound), **at the gate** (the numbers at the end of this file). Vendor blogs sell video work, so their numbers are their own; a rule here leans on them only where a maker or a study says the same.

## Structure: what the film says and in what order

### 1. Open on the payoff: frame 0 is a picture, the hook lands by 3 s

The first frame is already a strong image (it is also the autoplay thumbnail in a feed) and the promise or payoff is on screen within the first 2-3 seconds. No fade from black, no logo sting, no title slide first.

- "Lead with the promise, prove it fast. Open on the single benefit in the first few seconds, then spend the rest demonstrating it." (Moonb (Lorenzo Nicolini, co-founder), [source](https://www.moonb.io/blog/saas-product-demo-video))
- "It takes just a few seconds for a movie or series trailer to grab you – or to lose you." (Michel Wedel, Xuan Liu, Savannah Shi and Thales Teixeira (Smith School research, co-authored with Netflix, Harvard Business School and Santa Clara University), [source](https://rhsmith.umd.edu/research/can-robot-create-better-movie-trailers))

### 2. Rising arc: the strongest moment late, the music lifts into it

Scenes are ordered so intensity rises, and the key (longest, strongest) moment comes near the end rather than the start; the film leaves the viewer wanting a little more.

- "When the key scene (typically the longest scene) is placed toward the end of the comedic trailer or clip, happiness levels and watching intention both increased." (Michel Wedel, Xuan Liu, Savannah Wei Shi, Thales Teixeira (Journal of Marketing research on making clips), [source](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/519861))
- "While not all moments in the trailer or clip are expected to have a huge impact, the start, peak, end and trajectory of audio and emotions matters." (Michel Wedel, Xuan Liu, Savannah Wei Shi, Thales Teixeira (Journal of Marketing research on making clips), [source](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/519861))

### 3. One promise per film

Decide the one thing the viewer should take away and cut every shot that does not serve it. A feature tour is the failure mode.

- "In retrospect, simplifying the script was a huge win." (Allen Pike, [source](https://allenpike.com/2012/making-a-funky-app-video))
- "Every shot must advance one. A shot that advances none gets deleted." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))

### 4. A person the viewer recognises

Give the problem a face: one recurring character (a named persona, hands, a team) the target viewer sees themselves in. Recurring characters also carry brand memory.

- "We gave that frustration a face, then the break: the moment the idea finally gets to ship and shine." (POWER SHIFTER, [source](https://powershifter.com/studio/rapid-mvp-launch-video-power-shifter-studio-case-study))
- "We chose to tell the story through three very different small businesses, giving the product a real and recognizable world to live in." (Wix Motion Studio (Elior Karmani, motion design lead), [source](https://www.stashmedia.tv/?p=122261))

### 5. Brand early, short and more than once

The brand (name, mark or product) shows within the first 5 seconds and returns briefly in different scenes, rather than once at length.

- "Creatives that show both the brand and the main message within the first five seconds are 1.7x more likely to rank in the top 20% for purchase intent." (Meta (research conducted with Toluna), [source](https://bestmediainfo.com/insights/want-reels-ads-that-actually-work-meta-just-revealed-the-playbook-10866888))
- "Ads that show the brand more than once, in different scenes or positions, are 1.8x more likely to rank in the top 20% for purchase intent." (Meta (research conducted with Toluna), [source](https://bestmediainfo.com/insights/want-reels-ads-that-actually-work-meta-just-revealed-the-playbook-10866888))

### 6. Lead with a feeling, prove with the product

Viewers come for a feeling; the hero film can lead with it (a metaphor, a mood) and let the product scenes prove it, with specs left to their own film.

- "Audiences go to a movie for the emotions they expect to experience there – fear during a horror film, sadness during a drama, happiness during a comedy. And that’s what a trailer delivers, a sample of the emotional content of a movie." (Michel Wedel, Xuan Liu, Savannah Shi and Thales Teixeira (Smith School research, co-authored with Netflix, Harvard Business School and Santa Clara University), [source](https://rhsmith.umd.edu/research/can-robot-create-better-movie-trailers))
- "Oura went the other way and made a feeling-first hero, the sort I’ve collected in my brand video examples." (Lorenzo Nicolini (Moonb, a video studio), [source](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video))

### 7. Humour and personality where the brand allows

A joke holds attention to its punchline and drives emotional intensity; even serious AI brands (Anthropic's Super Bowl films) use it.

- "If you set up a joke, people will usually stick around for the punchline." (Rise Interactive (a Quad company) with Meta, recapping a Reels webinar, [source](https://www.riseinteractive.com/blog/language-of-reels-content-that-converts))
- "Promote right-brained features (such as sense of place, humour and hummable music) which drive emotional intensity and help support long-term ad effectiveness." (System1 (creative effectiveness platform) with ITV, [source](https://www.advanced-television.com/2023/07/06/research-emotion-humour-key-to-tv-ad-success))

### 8. Tension before the product

Open on the pain or the old way, then pivot to the product; do not open on a feature list.

- "The mistake I see most is a video that opens on the feature list, when the buyer only wants to know what changes for them." (Lorenzo Nicolini (Moonb, a video studio), [source](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video))
- "The concept was to use this quote to frame the situation and visualize these two types of companies." (Moonbase, [source](https://medium.com/process-and-craft/start-to-finish-making-a-startup-promo-video-5525e6c50485))

### 9. Show the product doing the job

The film shows the action, the in-between state and the result, instead of adjectives about the product; the product is on screen more than once.

- "It autoplays in the feed. It shows the product doing the thing instead of claiming it." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))
- "A twenty-second film shows the click, the incomplete state, the payment landing, and the status flipping to active." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))

### 10. Short: 30-60 s for the hero, every line earns its place

Hero launch films sit around a minute or less; music-only films run shorter than voiced ones; at that length every sentence and shot must have a purpose.

- "One minute. That’s less than the time it takes to brew a cup of tea or even wait at a red light." (Dropbox Brand Design team, [source](https://medium.com/dropbox-design/the-making-of-a-1-minute-dropbox-video-c0e909d98fc3))
- "1:30 isn’t actually a lot of time; each sentence must have a purpose and not be repetitive or extraneous." (Moonbase, [source](https://medium.com/process-and-craft/start-to-finish-making-a-startup-promo-video-5525e6c50485))

## Picture: how it looks and moves

### 11. Real product frames as proof (filmed, not invented)

Screens a buyer can log in to are shown as they are: real frames, cropped and lit and moved by a virtual camera, rather than an animator's copy. Invented UI is ruled out.

- "If your buyer can log in to the screen you show, record it. Don't redraw it." (ADVIDS Research (Advids), [source](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends))
- "The skill pulls the feature's Figma frames at 3x resolution and films them with a virtual camera: zoom into the action, whip-pan between screens, hold the payoff." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))

### 12. Every change has a visible cause and a sound on its frame

A press, a cursor or a typed key causes each change on screen, and its click sound lands on the exact frame of contact; without the cue the motion reads as disconnected.

- "A button can animate perfectly, but without a click landing at exactly the right moment, it often feels strangely disconnected." (Sam Jupp / Motion The Agency, [source](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/sound-design-examples-before-after-in-motion))
- "Quick takeaway is, without SFX, the toggle looks like it moves. With SFX, it feels like it clicks." (Sam Jupp / Motion The Agency, [source](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/sound-design-examples-before-after-in-motion))

### 13. Redraw the UI as motion graphics

Rebuild the interface as simple vector layers so each part can move, zoom without blur and stay clean of distracting text; update it when the product changes.

- "Screen recording is another option, but building out content in the real product takes almost as long as recreating the screens in Illustrator, it doesn’t give us as much control, and again it’s hard to avoid mistakes while recording." (Sofia Hoflin (author); Percolate in-house creative team, [source](https://medium.com/@sofiahoflin/how-we-make-product-films-in-house-1b911ccbec69))
- "They show the minimized version of the product without this extra text that's going to be distracting." (Renat Gabitov (SaaS Video Marketing, Represent studio), [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jtpXWRIZuGM))

### 14. Camera and depth over the UI

A 3D camera moves through tilted UI planes, layers at different depths move at different speeds, long lenses compress perspective.

- "And what will happen now also in the movement, you will see that there will be a bit more depth." (TomsProject (Fullstack Editor), [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jccbzpicst8))
- "and we'll make this the preset of 135 mm so that's a zoomed in focal length and that'll compress a lot of the perspective here" (Jake In Motion, [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bw-ucrydyuw))

### 15. Big type that moves with the picture

Few words, large, animated per word (slide up in place), placed on the product shot rather than stopping the film for a slide; the words say what the viewer gets.

- "If words carry your message, put them on the product shot. Don't stop the video for a slide." (ADVIDS Research (Advids), [source](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends))
- "You want to say no bouncing, no letters fading in. Each word just slides up in place one after the other." (Rourke, [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cDb-wPq0Iyk))

### 16. A handful of colours, one background tone

Fix a few brand colours (from the product's own design files) and use only those; keep one background tone so the eye does not readjust at each cut.

- "We selected a handful of colors and made sure to only use those colors in every touchpoint in the video." (Dropbox Brand Design team, [source](https://medium.com/dropbox-design/the-making-of-a-1-minute-dropbox-video-c0e909d98fc3))
- "If your product screens are light, keep the whole video light. A cut between light and dark makes the eye adjust every time." (ADVIDS Research (Advids), [source](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends))

### 17. Eased, staggered motion

Elements arrive staggered, never all at once; moves ease or ride springs and speed ramps; small offsets give character.

- "Now I'm also going to offset everything a bit because now everything comes in the screen at the same time and that always is a bit boring." (TomsProject (Fullstack Editor), [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Jccbzpicst8))
- "Now, just delay the layers so they don't start at the same time." (Joseph | Video Editing (Ultimate Editors), [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zWbLfFA9l8s))

## Editing: cuts, shots, pace

### 18. Carry the eye across cuts (match moves, match shapes)

A cut continues something from the shot before it: the camera keeps moving the same way, a shape becomes the next shape, the camera pulls out through a screen. Hard cuts between unrelated camera moves make the eye restart.

- "We decided instead to pull back, out through the screen, revealing the graphics to be occurring on the screen of the first company." (Moonbase, [source](https://medium.com/process-and-craft/start-to-finish-making-a-startup-promo-video-5525e6c50485))
- "you have to make sure that the camera movements of the stock footage clip are not completely opposite to your shots because then you won't be able to make a smooth transition" (Joris Hermans, [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OFAfxPlkv_8))

### 19. A pace curve, not one tempo

Vary shot length: bursts of quick cuts against held beats. Too many scenes or uniform fast cutting lowers liking; makers build rhythm between music, cuts and action.

- "A clip with a larger number of scenes resulted in a lower level of happiness, and consequently, a lower number of people who expressed a desire to see the film." (Michel Wedel, Xuan Liu, Savannah Wei Shi, Thales Teixeira (Journal of Marketing research on making clips), [source](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/519861))
- "The research finds that audiences generally don’t like comedy trailers to contain too many scenes or to be too fast-paced." (Michel Wedel, Xuan Liu, Savannah Shi and Thales Teixeira (Smith School research, co-authored with Netflix, Harvard Business School and Santa Clara University), [source](https://rhsmith.umd.edu/research/can-robot-create-better-movie-trailers))

### 20. Shots of 2-6 s, one idea each

Each shot holds one clear idea; studies and studios land at 2-3 s between cuts for music-driven edits and 3-6 s for explanatory animation.

- "The clips had a similar number of cuts (M = 13.33, SD = 2.14) leading to a time interval of 2–3 s between two consecutive cuts." (Chenyang Lin, Maggie Yeh, Ladan Shams (UCLA), [source](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1rq255xj/qt1rq255xj.pdf))
- "3–6 sper animated clip, so each shot holds one clear idea before the next page turns" (Maverick Frame Studio, [source](https://maverickframe.com/success-stories/ai-brand-film-digital-book))

### 21. Cut during the move and carry it across (cut on action)

A cut made while something moves hides the join: the next shot starts moving the same way at about the same speed, and the cut falls early in the move. A cut between two moves that do not match is the stutter viewers read as clunky.

- "An action cut is a cut made during movement, usually from one view of an action to another so the movement appears to continue." (How to Film School, [source](https://howtofilmschool.com/dictionary/action-cut-film-editing/))
- "editors tend to cut in the first third of the action" (Videomaker, [source](https://www.videomaker.com/article/c10/13536-cutting-on-action/))

### 22. Ease the move, then hold before the cut or the next move

A screen move starts and ends softly (120-200 ms eases), the viewer gets 250-400 ms or more of settled picture after the click or zoom, and only then the cut or the next move. Measured: library median hold after a move 0.08 s (p90 1.7 s); 16% of moves are eased at both ends.

- "Leave 250-400 ms of space after clicks or key moments before zooms, highlights, or overlays." (Clueso (screen-recording tool vendor), [source](https://www.clueso.io/blog/how-to-make-tasteful-screen-capture-videos))
- "Add soft ease-ins and outs (120-200 ms) so your zooms feel natural, not abrupt" (Clueso (screen-recording tool vendor), [source](https://www.clueso.io/blog/how-to-make-tasteful-screen-capture-videos))

### 23. Every screen change has a reason you can see

One action per shot, and each cut answers 'what changed on screen?': the cursor, a click, a zoom or a scroll leads into the new view, so the eye follows. Eye trace is one of Murch's six criteria.

- "Stick to one action per shot. Every cut should clearly answer: what changed on screen?" (Clueso (screen-recording tool vendor), [source](https://www.clueso.io/blog/how-to-make-tasteful-screen-capture-videos))
- "Eye-tracing is often used to orient a viewer's eyes across consecutive edits." (StudioBinder (Alyssa Maio), [source](https://www.studiobinder.com/blog/walter-murch-rule-of-six/))

### 24. Every shot long enough to read: no bursts of sub-second shots

A shot needs time to be read: library median shot 1.5 s, demo sections 1.9 s; 17% of library shots are under 0.6 s and a burst of more than 8 in a row happens in about 5% of films.

- "Leave 250-400 ms of space after clicks or key moments before zooms, highlights, or overlays." (Clueso (screen-recording tool vendor), [source](https://www.clueso.io/blog/how-to-make-tasteful-screen-capture-videos))
- "Stick to one action per shot. Every cut should clearly answer: what changed on screen?" (Clueso (screen-recording tool vendor), [source](https://www.clueso.io/blog/how-to-make-tasteful-screen-capture-videos))

## Sound: music, effects, voice

### 25. Pick the track to a tone brief, audition several

Music is chosen for the brand's tone (written as a few adjectives) and the space the picture needs, and auditioned against references, not taken as the first track that has a drop.

- "An energetic soundtrack can undermine a premium brand, while slow, cinematic music can make a fast-paced product demo feel sluggish." (Sam Jupp / Motion The Agency, [source](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/sound-design-examples-before-after-in-motion))
- "The track needed sparse and simple instrumentation to leave ample room for the voiceover." (Dropbox Brand Design team, [source](https://medium.com/dropbox-design/the-making-of-a-1-minute-dropbox-video-c0e909d98fc3))

### 26. Cut on the accented beat, not on every beat

Scene cuts land on the music's accented downbeats; viewers like the aligned version more without noticing why. Cutting on every obvious beat can hurt the edit.

- "Despite the subliminal nature of this manipulation, a higher perceptual pleasure was found for temporal congruency compared with incongruency." (Chenyang Lin, Maggie Yeh, Ladan Shams (UCLA), [source](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1rq255xj/qt1rq255xj.pdf))
- "the cuts in the video between scenes were aligned with the accented first beat of a measure in the soundtrack." (Chenyang Lin, Maggie Yeh, Ladan Shams (UCLA), [source](https://escholarship.org/content/qt1rq255xj/qt1rq255xj.pdf))

### 27. Music first, then cut the music to the picture

Choose the track early (by the animatic) and edit the track itself so its pauses and peak land on the picture's key moments.

- "always remember that you can cut and edit music so that it matches better with what's happening in your video" (Joris Hermans, [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=OFAfxPlkv_8))
- "We can find a song from here for our video which is actually usually done in the storyboarding that animatic stage" (Renat Gabitov (SaaS Video Marketing, Represent studio), [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jtpXWRIZuGM))

### 28. Sound design with roles and restraint

Music sets direction, ambience sets place, SFX mark single actions; not every movement gets a sound, and audio transitions are as smooth as the picture's.

- "Background music establishes the emotional direction of the video, ambience helps viewers feel like they're inside a real environment, and sound effects reinforce individual actions and interactions." (Sam Jupp / Motion The Agency, [source](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/sound-design-examples-before-after-in-motion))
- "More isn't always better." (Sam Jupp / Motion The Agency, [source](https://www.motiontheagency.com/blog/sound-design-examples-before-after-in-motion))

### 29. No voice by default; a flat voice is worse than none

Launch films for AI and software brands increasingly run on music and type; when a film plays muted in a feed it is written for the eye. A weak voiceover costs more polish than it adds.

- "Every Anthropic and OpenAI video from April has no voice." (ADVIDS Research (Advids), [source](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends))
- "No voiceover. No stock footage. No abstract 3D shapes. The camera moves through the real product, one action at a time." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))

### 30. If there is a voice: a cast human, for walkthroughs

Keep a voice when the film walks through a task; cast it to a written tone brief, compare takes, and say so on screen if it is synthetic.

- "Keep it if the video walks through a task." (ADVIDS Research (Advids), [source](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends))
- "She should sound like a smart friend who wants to share ways to make your life better." (Dropbox Brand Design team, [source](https://medium.com/dropbox-design/the-making-of-a-1-minute-dropbox-video-c0e909d98fc3))

### 31. Reads on mute

Most first views are muted: the story is carried by picture and on-screen words, planned from the start, not captions bolted on at the end.

- "Whatever you pick, design the demo to still make sense on mute, because most first views happen with the sound off." (Moonb (Lorenzo Nicolini, co-founder), [source](https://www.moonb.io/blog/saas-product-demo-video))
- "nobody thinks about sound-off viewing until captions get bolted on at the end" (Lorenzo Nicolini (Moonb, a video studio), [source](https://www.moonb.io/blog/product-launch-video))

### 32. Cut to the phrase of the music, not to every beat

The beat tells you a cut is possible; the phrase (a bar, two bars, a chord change, a drum entrance) tells you why it belongs there. Cut once a bar or every two bars and skip beats in between.

- "you never want to cut your film too closely to the beat of the track." (PremiumBeat (Shutterstock), [source](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/how-to-edit-a-film-to-music-without-it-becoming-a-music-video))
- "Doing it once or twice in a sequence can work well, but doing it on every cut will start to feel redundant in most cases." (PremiumBeat (Shutterstock), [source](https://www.premiumbeat.com/blog/how-to-edit-a-film-to-music-without-it-becoming-a-music-video))

### 33. Let the sound cross the cut (J and L cuts)

The next shot's sound starts before its picture (J cut) or the last shot's sound runs past it (L cut), so the cut carries momentum instead of a stop.

- "a J cut, so named because the clip looks like a little "J" in the timeline, is when the audio of the next shot precedes the video" (No Film School, [source](https://nofilmschool.com/2017/10/editing-101-what-are-j-and-l-cuts-and-why-should-you-be-using-them))
- "J cuts make great scene transitions." (No Film School, [source](https://nofilmschool.com/2017/10/editing-101-what-are-j-and-l-cuts-and-why-should-you-be-using-them))

## Process: how to work

### 34. Animatic first, on the final music, checked on mute

Before any finished art, a low-fidelity full-length render with real timing, camera and music; it answers rhythm, eye-line and 'does it read on mute' while changes are cheap. Makers find the animatic ends up matching the final edit.

- "The animatic is a complete render. Low fidelity on purpose." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))
- "The animatic answers the expensive questions early: does the rhythm work, does the eye land in the right place, and is the story understandable on mute?" (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))

### 35. Start from reference films

Pick a benchmark (Lago picked Linear's launch films) and study films from similar and adjacent companies before designing; the bar is the reference, not a blank page.

- "Our reference was Linear's launch films." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))
- "The best way to do this is to look at cool references of similar companies, companies on adjacent niches." (Renat Gabitov (SaaS Video Marketing, Represent studio), [source](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jtpXWRIZuGM))

### 36. Corrections go into the pipeline, not the prompt

Every note from a review is encoded once in the reusable pipeline (skill, scene patterns, defaults), so review rounds shrink film over film.

- "A prompt does not compound. A skill does." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))
- "Six videos in, feedback dropped from a dozen rounds to about one." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))

## Distribution: formats and release

### 37. One film, many cuts: :60/:30/:15 and a silent master

A launch film ships as a package: the hero master plus shorter cuts made by dropping whole beats (planned in the script so they can be dropped), a silent version and a poster frame.

- "The output is not one file. It includes the 16:9 master, a silent master, 1:1, 4:5, and 9:16 recompositions, a poster frame, a preview, and a checksum manifest." (Anh-Tho Chuong (Lago), [source](https://getlago.com/blog/we-killed-our-motion-design-job))
- "She structured the cuts in a way that allowed us to omit full sentences and sections to create the shorter variations." (Sofia Hoflin (author); Percolate in-house creative team, [source](https://medium.com/@sofiahoflin/how-we-make-product-films-in-house-1b911ccbec69))

### 38. Vertical 9:16 recomposed, not cropped

Feeds on phones (Reels, Shorts, TikTok, X on mobile) want 9:16. Makers build a vertical version with its own layout and close-ups, not a centre crop of the 16:9 master.

- "Meta urges advertisers to build specifically for the Reels environment—vertical 9:16, sound-on viewing and fast hooks—rather than simply cropping or reusing existing video assets." (Meta (research conducted with Toluna), [source](https://bestmediainfo.com/insights/want-reels-ads-that-actually-work-meta-just-revealed-the-playbook-10866888))
- "Research has shown that Reels ads that featured 9:16 videos showed a +7-point higher average positive response score compared to non-9:16 ads." (Booyah Advertising (Madison Meagher) with Rohith Bhatia (Meta), [source](https://www.booyah.com/blog/why-you-should-use-creative-designed-specifically-for-reels))

### 39. A release plan: native upload, quiet slot, the first hour

The film is the anchor of a release: teased before, uploaded natively, posted in a less crowded slot, the first hour worked; it also lives where the decision happens (homepage, signup).

- "The first 60 minutes of any launch are the most important window you'll get." (Subah Wadhwani (Atomik Growth / The Launch Video Company), [source](https://www.atomikgrowth.com/blog/how-we-got-3-6m-views-9-000-demos-on-a-monday-morning-launch-x))
- "A less crowded feed beats a peak feed you can't stand out in." (Subah Wadhwani (Atomik Growth / The Launch Video Company), [source](https://www.atomikgrowth.com/blog/how-we-got-3-6m-views-9-000-demos-on-a-monday-morning-launch-x))

## Ending

### 40. End card: logo briefly, then one ask

Close on the logo, but not for long, and give the viewer one thing to do (a URL, 'book a demo', 'try it'). A menu of options or a long logo hold loses them.

- "If your video ends in a feed with no voice to close it, end on your logo. Then decide what that screen asks the buyer to do." (ADVIDS Research (Advids), [source](https://advids.co/blog/enterprise-ai-launch-video-trends))
- "Showing or voicing a CTA increases the likelihood of ranking in the top 20% for purchase intent by 1.9x." (Meta (research conducted with Toluna), [source](https://bestmediainfo.com/insights/want-reels-ads-that-actually-work-meta-just-revealed-the-playbook-10866888))

## AI products specifically

### 41. AI products: show the agent working and what it made

For an agent, the demo is the chat, the steps or tool calls, the trace and the artifact it produced; the film follows moments of progress, not a list of features.

- "What works is showing the chat, the tool calls, the reasoning trace, the artifact it produced." (AutoAE (author Keston Collins), [source](https://autoae.online/blog/how-to-make-ai-agent-launch-video-without-after-effects))
- "the focus stays on moments of progress rather than on individual features or tasks." (Wix Motion Studio (Elior Karmani, motion design lead), [source](https://www.stashmedia.tv/?p=122261))

### 42. AI products: real output, checked before release

Every answer on screen is real and fact-checked; a wrong answer in a pre-filmed demo becomes the story (Humane), and invented UI reads as fake.

- "Unbelievably, this happened in a pre-filmed demo that obviously wasn’t fact-checked." (D. Griffin Jones (Cult of Mac), [source](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/humane-ai-pin-is-a-total-waste))
- "The AI gave completely wrong answers and provided no way to check their accuracy." (D. Griffin Jones (Cult of Mac), [source](https://www.cultofmac.com/news/humane-ai-pin-is-a-total-waste))

### 43. AI products: no robots, brains or neon

Keep AI from being the visual hero: no neural-network stock, gradient blobs, robot faces or neon tech; the agent's work is the image.

- "A big part of the idea was to make the agents feel active without turning AI itself into the visual hero." (Wix Motion Studio (Elior Karmani, motion design lead), [source](https://www.stashmedia.tv/?p=122261))
- "Stock graphics of neural networks, generic gradient blobs, a robot face: none of that signals that your agent works." (AutoAE (author Keston Collins), [source](https://autoae.online/blog/how-to-make-ai-agent-launch-video-without-after-effects))

## The gate's numbers

`film.py gate` measures these on every cut and compares them with the previous cut; they come from measuring published films the same way (see `data/reference-numbers.json`, `data/cut-limits.json`).

| Measure | Rule | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Mean shot length | Not more than 5% longer than the previous cut's (a hard stop only with 8+ cuts or a `--timeline`) | A cut that got slower reads as a step back |
| Share of static frames | Not more than 3 points above the previous cut's | A held frame with nothing moving is the commonest reason a film feels dead |
| Words per card | Warn above 1.25x the previous cut's | Cards are read in a glance; more words than that is a document |
| Burst of tiny shots | Stop on more than 8 shots in a row under 0.6 s | Library 95th percentile; a burst belongs to the end of a film |
| Tiny shots overall | Warn above 38% of shots under 0.6 s | Library 90th percentile |
| Cuts on every beat | Stop on more than 6 cuts in a row exactly one beat apart | Cut to the phrase, not every beat |
| Loudness | -14 LUFS, true peak -1 dBTP (the mixer does it) | Platforms normalise to about this; anything louder is turned down, anything quieter is not turned up |
| Notes | Every note the owner gave has a tick with a place in this exact file | What they said binds every film that follows |
